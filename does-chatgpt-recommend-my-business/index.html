Does ChatGPT recommend my business?

Published 26 October 2026.

Short answer. Only a live ChatGPT answer can tell you, and the next answer can differ. There is no public list of businesses that are on ChatGPT, and no setting that adds yours.

You can check in one sitting. Ask the way a customer would: do not type your business name, do not say you are the owner, start a fresh chat, put your city in the question, and turn web search on. Repeat each of those prompts about three times, write down who was named and which sources were cited, and keep a separate question that asks for your business by name so you can check the phone and website. Then read the pattern as named consistently, named sometimes, or not at all. OpenAI's search help article says placement is not guaranteed, and that an answer can be incomplete, out of date, or wrong.

More customers now ask AI assistants which local business to hire. This page is the check. When you are ready to change the pages and listings a search can read, use the longer guide, how to get your business recommended by ChatGPT. How to check if ChatGPT recommends your business

Set aside about 30 to 45 minutes. You need the service in the words a customer would use, the city and state you serve, one job people pay you for, a ZIP or neighborhood, and your business name spelled the way it is on your website. Have a notes file open. The blank log is below.

  1. Ask as a customer, in a fresh chat. Open chatgpt.com. Use a signed-out window if you can. OpenAI says web search is available without an account, and that saved memories, when they are enabled, can be added into the query ChatGPT sends onward. A new chat for every run, including the repeats, keeps an earlier answer from steering the next one. Do not say that you own the business or that this is a test.
  2. Turn Search on, and turn a VPN off. Before you send, open View all tools and choose Search, or type / and choose Search there. OpenAI says ChatGPT can start a search on its own when the question needs current information. Choose it yourself so this sitting is not a guess. A VPN can move the rough location taken from your IP address, so switch the VPN off. Sharing a precise device location is optional and stays off until you enable it, which is why the city belongs in the prompt.
  3. Put the city in the question, and leave your name out. Discovery prompts are the ones a customer asks when they do not know you. If the businesses in the answer are in another metro, OpenAI says to include the city, neighborhood, or postal code and ask again. Do that in a new chat, and note the wrong-city try in the log. Do not keep chatting in the thread that already went to the wrong place.
  4. Repeat each discovery prompt about three times. Same words, new chat, Search on, once per chat. About three runs is enough to see a pattern in this sitting. It is still a small sample. Send the prompt once and keep that reply. Regenerating until your name shows up, then logging only the later reply, makes the sample unfair.
  5. Compare Search on and Search off for one prompt. Use the first discovery prompt only. One new chat with Search on, one new chat with Search off, same words. The Search-on reply is the recommendation test. The Search-off reply shows what you get without a web search. Do not mix those rows when you score the pattern.
  6. Write down names and sources, then open two links. For each Search-on answer, record every business the customer is told to contact, and the domain of each citation. A domain is enough: a company's site, a directory, or a review page. Open Sources when that control is on the reply. OpenAI says citations can be selected to open the source, and that those results can be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect, so the page you open has to back the sentence. Open two sources per Search-on discovery answer. If the reply shows neither a citation nor Sources, leave it out of the pattern. Ask the same words once more in a new chat with Search on, score that later reply, and note that the first try was not a web result.
  7. Run the branded check last, in its own chat. After the discovery runs, ask for your business by name and compare the phone, city, and website in the answer with your website. A right answer here means you were findable when someone typed your name. It is not a recommendation. Keep it off the "named consistently" count.

Named consistently, sometimes, or not at all

Count only the nine Search-on discovery rows (runs 1 to 9), and count only a yes. Leave out the Search-off row and the branded row. A mentioned row is not a yes.

Named consistently

Your business was one to call on every Search-on discovery run in this sitting. Save the date and the log. This is a snapshot from one session, not a fixed place. OpenAI says placement is not guaranteed, and results can be incomplete or out of date. Ask the same prompts again later, in new chats. Until then, keep one public version of your name, phone, city, and services. If a cited source showed an old phone, correct that listing. The guide's section on what to fix next walks through the pages and listings. Doing that work does not guarantee the next answer will name you.

Named sometimes

You were a business to call on some Search-on discovery runs and not on others. Sort the log by prompt. If the job prompt missed you, check that a page on your site says, in normal sentences, that you do that job in that city. If the neighborhood prompt missed you, check that your site states the area you serve. Open the sources on the runs that named other businesses, and note any directory or review page that describes them and not you. Three runs can disagree even when you changed nothing, so "sometimes" means a mixed snapshot. Use the same fix section in the guide for the longer next step.

Not at all

None of the Search-on discovery runs named you as a business to call. Read the branded row before you change anything.

  • If that branded answer could not find you, or it got your phone, city, or website wrong, check crawler access and the words on your homepage. Open your domain's robots.txt file and look for a block on OAI-SearchBot, or a block on every robot. OpenAI's crawler docs say opting out of OAI-SearchBot keeps a site out of ChatGPT search answers. A bare link and title can still be shown. The search help article says eligibility depends on that crawler being allowed to fetch the site, and on the host allowing traffic from the published searchbot IP addresses. Give a robots.txt edit about a day before you test again. Allowing the crawler does not guarantee a recommendation. GPTBot is the training crawler, a separate switch from search. ChatGPT-User may fetch a page because a person asked, and OpenAI says it is not the control for search inclusion. On your own homepage, the name, city, phone, and service need to be readable text, not only a logo. The guide has the lines to look for in robots.txt. If you add an allow rule, leave the other rules your site already needs.
  • If the branded answer found you and the phone and website matched, people who already know your name can be pointed at you, and the open questions still did not recommend you. Compare who was named and which domains were cited. Then use what to fix next in the guide. One sitting is not proof that you can never be named.

What this sitting cannot prove

  • About three repeats is a small sample from one hour and one wording. A different sentence can produce a different list.
  • OpenAI says search results and citations can be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect. Open the sources rather than trusting the summary alone.
  • The Search-off row is not the recommendation test. Score the pattern from Search-on discovery rows only.
  • Answers move over time. See AI's #1 local pick changed in about half of markets in one month.
  • A Maps ranking does not, by itself, mean ChatGPT will recommend you. The guide's Maps section summarizes two October 2026 checks. NamedScan does not see a private ranking score inside ChatGPT. We do not promise or guarantee rankings or recommendations.

What OpenAI's docs say

Checked 26 October 2026. Where this page describes how ChatGPT search behaves, it stays inside what the OpenAI documents below say.

  • Searching the web with ChatGPT covers who can use web search (Free, Go, Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu, and people who are signed out), how a cited source is opened, and how an approximate location can come from the IP address. It also explains that ChatGPT may rewrite the question for another search provider and share a general location, without sending the IP address itself or the ChatGPT account. Memories can change that rewritten query. The article says placement is not guaranteed, and that a site is eligible when the search crawler can fetch it and the host allows the published searchbot IP addresses.
  • Overview of OpenAI Crawlers separates the bots. OAI-SearchBot is the one that can put a site into a ChatGPT search answer. Opting out leaves the site out of those answers, while a navigational link can remain. OpenAI's recommendation is to allow OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt and to allow the published IP addresses. Plan on about 24 hours after a robots.txt edit. GPTBot can be blocked at the same time. It is for training, not for this search decision. ChatGPT-User is a visit triggered by a person's question. OpenAI says it does not decide search inclusion, and that robots.txt may not cover that visit.

Allowing OAI-SearchBot makes pages eligible to be quoted. It does not mean ChatGPT will recommend the business.

Is my business on ChatGPT?

There is no directory of businesses that are on ChatGPT. ChatGPT can name a business when web search finds public information it can use. OpenAI says a site that blocks the OAI-SearchBot crawler is not shown in ChatGPT search answers, and that allowing the crawler does not guarantee placement. Ask for your business by name, with Search on, and see whether the answer finds you and cites a source. That is a findability check, not a recommendation.

Why repeat the same prompt three times?

One answer can differ from the next even when you change nothing. About three runs, each in a new chat, with the same wording, is enough to see whether you were named consistently, sometimes, or not at all in that sitting. It is still a small sample. It does not predict what ChatGPT will say tomorrow.

What is the difference between a recommendation and a correct description?

A recommendation is when a discovery answer names your business as one to contact, even though you did not type your name. A correct description is when a later question that does include your name returns the right phone, city, and website. You can be described correctly and still be missing from the open questions. Count only the search-on discovery runs when you decide consistently, sometimes, or not at all. If your name only appears in passing, and the answer does not tell the customer to contact you, that run is not a yes.

Does a high Google Maps rank mean ChatGPT recommends me?

No. A Maps rank does not, by itself, mean ChatGPT will name you when a customer asks. Run the self-check on this page to see what ChatGPT answered.
